❓ Common Owner Questions
"Can I just leave this to my kids?"
They can — but the obligation transfers with the deed. Most adult children will owe fees immediately after the owner's death.
"If I stop paying, can they sue me?"
Yes, but in practice most resorts pursue collection and credit reporting before litigation. Key: what state is the deed recorded in?
"I paid $38,000 for this — I can't just walk away."
Sunk cost objection. Reframe: "The question isn't what you paid — it's what you'll pay over the next 10 years to keep something you're not using."
"Can't I sell it through Wyndham?"
Wyndham's resale program (Ovation) returns no money — it's a deed-back, not a sale. ROFR means no third-party buyer ever completes.
"What about renting it out?"
Rental income rarely covers maintenance fees. Third-party rental programs are frequently scams. Address this directly.

📚 Frequently Asked Questions
"What is a timeshare maintenance fee and why does it increase?"
Maintenance fees cover HOA operating costs. Increases are voted on by the HOA board — owners have no direct veto. FL §721.15 governs annual budget disclosure requirements.
"What does Wyndham's Ovation program actually do?"
Ovation is a deed-back program — not a sale. The owner receives nothing in return. Eligibility requires: loan paid in full, no delinquent fees, account in good standing. Wait times vary from 6 to 24 months.
"If I default, what happens?"
Wyndham typically pursues: (1) collections agency referral, (2) negative credit reporting, (3) possible foreclosure on the deeded interest. Timeline: 30–90 days to collections, 6–18 months to foreclosure in FL. Credit impact: 100–200 pt drop.
⚖️ State Law — Florida
📋FL §721.10: 10-calendar-day rescission period from contract execution OR receipt of public offering statement, whichever is later
📋FL §721.15: HOA must provide annual budget to owners. Special assessments require 60-day notice.
📋FL §721.855: Timeshare plan termination — developer can vote to terminate if 100% of interests are conveyed or abandoned
⚠️Key note: FL foreclosure is judicial. A contested foreclosure can take 3–5 years. This affects exit timelines.
